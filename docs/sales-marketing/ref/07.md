# Phase 07: Sales Channels, Commissions, and Incentives

## Role and Implementation Approach

Act as a senior ERP architect, business analyst, compensation-system designer, database architect, backend engineer, frontend engineer, and QA engineer.

Implement **Phase 07: Sales Channels, Commissions, and Incentives** in the existing ERP repository.

This ERP is intended for use across multiple industries. All channel models, commission rules, incentive plans, and payout workflows must be configurable and industry-agnostic. Do not introduce assumptions specific to garment manufacturing, textiles, or any particular industry.

This is one phase of a larger Sales & Marketing implementation. Extend the existing application and reuse its established architecture, domain services, integrations, and security model.

## 1. Phase Objective

Build a comprehensive Sales Channels, Commissions, and Incentives capability that enables authorized users to:

- Define and manage sales channels and channel classifications.
- Associate sales transactions with their originating or responsible channels.
- Define commission plans and configurable calculation rules.
- Assign commission plans to eligible representatives, teams, or other supported participants.
- Calculate commissions from authoritative sales and financial data.
- Support fixed, percentage-based, tiered, and other explicitly supported commission structures.
- Manage incentive plans, eligibility, achievement rules, and rewards.
- Track earned, pending, approved, adjusted, reversed, and settled commission amounts.
- Review commission statements and incentive attainment.
- Handle cancellations, returns, credit notes, refunds, and other adjustments.
- Maintain auditable calculation results and historical plan versions.
- Integrate with existing Finance or Payroll systems for settlement without duplicating their responsibilities.
- Analyze performance by sales channel, representative, team, territory, and other supported dimensions.

The system must make compensation calculations transparent, reproducible, and auditable.

**Calculated commission, earned commission, approved payable amounts, and actual payments are distinct states.** Do not treat them as interchangeable.

## 7. Commission Calculation Engine

Implement commission calculations through a shared, testable service with clearly defined inputs, rules, outputs, and calculation provenance.

Do not spread commission formulas across controllers, UI components, and reporting queries.

### 7.1 Supported calculation methods

Where applicable, support:

- Fixed amount per eligible transaction.
- Percentage of an eligible transaction value.
- Percentage of eligible margin, only if reliable cost and margin data exists.
- Quantity-based commission, where relevant.
- Tiered commission rates.
- Threshold-based commission.
- Capped or minimum commission.
- Split commission between eligible participants, if required and explicitly configured.
- Flat or stepped incentive rates, where they are commission-plan components.

Implement only the methods that can be represented and tested reliably.

Do not create an unrestricted formula execution engine. Use a constrained, validated rule model.

### 7.2 Commission basis

Each plan must explicitly define the amount on which commission is calculated.

Potential bases include:

- Eligible booked Sales Order value.
- Eligible invoiced value.
- Eligible collected payment value.
- Eligible quantity.
- Eligible margin, where available.
- Another explicitly supported metric.

Do not treat order value, invoiced value, recognized revenue, and collected cash as interchangeable.

If a commission plan is based on collected payments, do not calculate the final earned amount as if the entire order value had already been collected.

For partial payments, calculate eligible commission according to the plan's documented rules.

### 7.3 Calculation order

Define and document a consistent calculation sequence. Where relevant, it may include:

1. Identify the eligible source transaction or event.
2. Validate the transaction status and effective dates.
3. Resolve the applicable plan version.
4. Resolve participant eligibility and attribution.
5. Determine the commission basis and eligible amount.
6. Apply exclusions and adjustments.
7. Apply thresholds and tiers.
8. Apply caps, minimums, and split rules.
9. Apply documented rounding and currency rules.
10. Persist the calculation details and resulting status.
11. Apply the required review and approval workflow.

The actual sequence must be explicit and consistent with the configured rule semantics.

### 7.4 Tiered commission rules

If tiered commissions are supported, define whether tiers use:

- Progressive or marginal rates, where each rate applies only to the portion within its tier.
- Retroactive or all-units rates, where achieving a tier changes the rate applied to the entire eligible basis.

Do not treat these approaches as interchangeable.

Validate tier boundaries, gaps, overlaps, threshold inclusivity, ordering, and applicable caps.

Provide clear examples in documentation and automated tests.

### 7.5 Commission calculation records

Each calculation must retain sufficient details to reproduce and audit the result, including:

- Source transaction or event reference.
- Eligible participant or participants.
- Applied plan and version.
- Applicable rule identifiers.
- Commission basis and eligible amount.
- Applied rate or tier.
- Adjustments and exclusions.
- Currency.
- Calculation timestamp.
- Calculation result.
- Rounding method.
- Current lifecycle status.
- Relevant source revision or snapshot reference.

Preserve historical calculations. Do not overwrite the only record of a previous calculation when rules or source records change.

## 9. Commission Lifecycle, Adjustments, and Reversals

Implement a clear lifecycle for calculated commission amounts.

Depending on the business rules, statuses may include:

- Calculated.
- Pending qualification.
- Pending review.
- Approved.
- On hold.
- Payable.
- Partially settled.
- Settled.
- Reversed.
- Cancelled.

Use the existing workflow conventions and avoid creating redundant statuses.

### 9.1 Qualification rules

Commission eligibility may depend on events or conditions such as:

- Order confirmation.
- Invoice issuance.
- Payment collection.
- Expiration of a return or cancellation window.
- Completion of another explicitly configured business condition.

Do not assume all commissions become payable at order confirmation.

The commission plan must define when an amount is calculated, earned, approved, and eligible for settlement.

### 9.2 Adjustments

Support authorized adjustments where required, such as:

- Correcting a source-data error.
- Recalculating after an eligible transaction amendment.
- Applying a documented commission adjustment.
- Handling partial refunds or credit notes.
- Correcting attribution or participant splits.

Adjustments must record the reason, actor, timestamp, source reference, and financial effect.

Do not silently overwrite historical commission records.

### 9.3 Cancellations, returns, and reversals

Define how the system handles:

- Cancelled Sales Orders.
- Cancelled invoices.
- Partial returns.
- Full returns.
- Credit notes.
- Refunds.
- Reversed payments.
- Duplicate source events.
- Transactions that change after commission approval or settlement.

Use compensating entries or explicit reversal records where appropriate.

Never delete a settled commission merely to hide a reversal.

A reversal must reference the original commission or settlement where possible.

Avoid reversing more commission than the original eligible amount, unless an explicitly authorized correction process permits it.

### 9.4 Recalculation

Provide a safe mechanism for recalculating eligible commissions when source data changes.

Requirements:

- Preserve prior calculation history.
- Detect whether the source event has already been processed.
- Apply the correct plan version.
- Create adjustments or replacement calculations according to the lifecycle.
- Avoid double-paying commissions after retries.
- Prevent unauthorized retroactive changes.
- Support reconciliation of commission records against authoritative source data.
