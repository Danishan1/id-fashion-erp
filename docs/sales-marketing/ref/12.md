# Phase 12: Promotions, Offers, and Campaign Budgets

## 1. Objective

Implement a production-ready, industry-agnostic **Promotions, Offers, and Campaign Budgets** capability within the existing Sales & Marketing ERP module.

The module must enable authorized users to:

- Define and manage promotional offers.
- Configure promotion eligibility, conditions, benefits, validity periods, and usage restrictions.
- Reuse the authoritative pricing and discount engine from Phase 03.
- Associate offers with marketing campaigns, channels, and eligible audiences.
- Plan, approve, allocate, monitor, and reconcile marketing budgets.
- Track planned, committed, actual, and remaining budget amounts.
- Integrate with existing Finance, Sales Orders, and campaign execution infrastructure.
- Audit changes to promotional rules and budget allocations.

The solution must remain industry-agnostic. Do not introduce garment-, textile-, manufacturing-, or other industry-specific assumptions, fields, or workflows.

**Core principle:** Sales pricing, promotional eligibility, and marketing expenditure are related but distinct concepts. Promotions must reuse the authoritative pricing engine, while campaign budgets must use existing financial records and accounting rules wherever available.

## 4. Promotion and Offer Management

Create a reusable promotion and offer management capability.

A promotion represents a defined commercial incentive or offer that may be made available to an eligible audience, campaign, channel, or transaction.

Support appropriate metadata, such as:

- Promotion name.
- Unique promotion code.
- Description and business objective.
- Promotion type.
- Owner or responsible team.
- Status.
- Start and end date/time.
- Time zone.
- Applicable channels.
- Applicable audience or eligibility rules.
- Priority.
- Usage limits.
- Approval information.
- Linked campaign references.
- Version and change history.
- Created and updated timestamps.
- Tenant or organizational scope.

Use the existing application conventions for identifiers, ownership, status, and tenancy.

Potential promotion types include:

- Percentage discount.
- Fixed-amount discount.
- Quantity-based incentive.
- Threshold-based offer.
- Bundle or combination offer, where supported by the pricing architecture.
- Free-shipping or other non-price benefit, if the existing commercial model supports it.
- Custom promotion types through a controlled extension interface.

These are possible types, not a requirement to implement every type regardless of existing support.

Do not implement a separate price calculation engine for promotions.

## 5. Promotion Lifecycle and Versioning

Support a clear, server-enforced lifecycle. Adapt statuses to existing conventions, such as:

- Draft.
- Pending approval.
- Approved.
- Scheduled.
- Active.
- Paused.
- Expired.
- Rejected.
- Archived.

Enforce valid transitions on the server.

Requirements:

- Validate required fields before submission or activation.
- Prevent invalid or expired promotions from being applied.
- Require appropriate approval before activation where policy requires it.
- Record activation, pause, expiry, and archival history.
- Prevent unauthorized changes to active promotions.
- Use a new version or revision when a change affects commercial behavior.
- Preserve the promotion version used in an applied pricing calculation.
- Ensure edits to a promotion do not silently rewrite previously issued quotations, accepted orders, or completed transactions.
- Define how scheduled promotions become active using existing scheduling infrastructure.
- Avoid duplicate activation events and concurrent lifecycle transitions.

Do not assume that setting a campaign to active automatically activates every associated promotion. Make the relationship and activation behavior explicit.

Supported conditions may include:

- Promotion validity period.
- Applicable sales or marketing channel.
- Eligible audience or customer classification.
- Minimum transaction value.
- Minimum quantity.
- Eligible product or service references, if supported by the existing pricing model.
- Eligible price lists.
- Selected customer or account references, where authorized.
- Existing campaign association.
- Usage count or redemption limits.
- Existing customer eligibility attributes.
- Explicit exclusions.
- Other conditions supported by existing domain services.

Only use authoritative fields and data sources already available. Do not create duplicate CRM or product fields merely to support a promotion.

### Rule requirements

- Validate rules on the server.
- Use allowlisted fields and operators.
- Use parameterized queries and existing safe query builders.
- Prevent arbitrary SQL or code execution.
- Reject unsupported rule types.
- Define behavior when data is missing.
- Enforce tenant and access restrictions.
- Avoid unbounded queries or uncontrolled evaluation complexity.
- Explain why an offer is eligible or ineligible where practical.

Eligibility must be evaluated consistently by the authoritative backend, not trusted from client-side calculations.

## 7. Benefits and Pricing Integration

Integrate promotional benefits with the existing Phase 03 pricing and discount engine.

**The existing pricing engine remains the single authoritative source for price and discount calculations.**

Requirements:

- Represent promotional benefits as supported inputs to the existing pricing engine.
- Reuse existing price lists, currency handling, quantity tiers, discount rules, and rounding behavior.
- Apply a clearly defined order of operations consistent with the pricing architecture.
- Define whether promotions may combine with other discounts.
- Prevent double application of the same benefit.
- Support explicit promotion priorities and stacking rules.
- Validate minimum and maximum discount limits where supported.
- Preserve price and discount calculation provenance.
- Return a structured explanation of the promotion and pricing outcome.
- Distinguish a promotional discount from a manually applied discount.
- Preserve the relevant promotion and pricing versions used in a calculation.

Do not duplicate tax calculations. Reuse the authoritative tax calculation mechanism if present, and make the applicable tax treatment explicit.

### Stacking and precedence

Define configurable, deterministic rules for:

- Whether multiple promotions can apply together.
- Whether a promotion excludes other promotions.
- Whether existing discounts may combine with a promotion.
- Priority when several promotions are eligible.
- Whether the highest-benefit rule or an explicit priority rule governs selection.
- Maximum discount or total benefit constraints.
- Handling of rounding and allocation across line items.

Do not leave stacking behavior to incidental query order or frontend logic.

If the existing pricing engine cannot safely support a requested promotion type, implement only the types that can be integrated correctly and document the remaining limitation.

## 8. Redemption, Usage Limits, and Concurrency

Where promotions are applied to transactions or redeemed through a supported workflow, track usage without introducing a duplicate order or payment system.

Support applicable controls such as:

- Maximum total redemptions.
- Maximum redemptions per eligible customer or account.
- Minimum and maximum transaction values.
- One-time-use restrictions.
- Validity-window enforcement.
- Usage reservations where a transaction workflow requires them.
- Release of reservations when the authoritative transaction is cancelled or abandoned.
- Reversal handling when a transaction is reversed, returned, or refunded, according to configured business policy.

### Concurrency requirements

Prevent simultaneous requests from exceeding redemption limits.

Use transactional counters, uniqueness constraints, locks, or other reliable concurrency controls appropriate to the existing architecture.

Distinguish between:

- A promotion being evaluated.
- A promotion being reserved.
- A promotion being applied to an authoritative transaction.
- A completed redemption.
- A reversed or released redemption.

Do not mark a promotion as redeemed solely because it appeared in a quotation preview.

If a quotation or order is abandoned, do not leave permanent usage consumption unless the documented business rule explicitly requires it.

Ensure retries and duplicate events cannot create duplicate redemption records.

## 12. Planned, Committed, Actual, and Remaining Amounts

Define each budget measure precisely.

At minimum:

- **Planned:** the proposed amount entered during planning.
- **Approved:** the authorized budget limit.
- **Allocated:** the portion assigned to a campaign, channel, or supported sub-budget.
- **Committed:** expenditure obligations recognized from authoritative records, such as approved purchase orders or other supported commitments.
- **Actual:** expenditure recognized from authoritative financial records according to the configured accounting basis.
- **Remaining:** the amount available under the defined budget policy.

Define the formulas and accounting basis used for each measure.

For example, a simple remaining-budget measure may be calculated as approved budget minus the applicable committed amount and actual expenditure not already included in that commitment measure. The exact formula must avoid double-counting and must be explicitly documented.

Do not assume that every ERP's financial data uses the same commitment or actual-expenditure semantics.

### Financial integrity requirements

- Use the authoritative currency and financial amount types.
- Avoid floating-point arithmetic for monetary values.
- Reuse existing currency and exchange-rate services.
- Define how multi-currency budgets are handled.
- Do not sum different currencies without a defined conversion basis.
- Preserve the source currency, converted amount, exchange-rate reference, and applicable date when conversion is necessary.
- Reuse existing tax and accounting policies.
- Distinguish committed costs from actual costs.
- Prevent duplicate recognition of the same financial transaction.
- Handle reversals and corrections through authoritative source records.
- Preserve historical budget snapshots or audit history as required by existing policy.

Never create fabricated expense records to make budget reporting appear complete.

- Purchase orders.

- Approved expense claims.
- Accounts payable transactions.
- General ledger entries.
- Existing financial budget or cost-center records.
- Other approved expenditure sources already present in the ERP.

Marketing provider delivery records from Phase 10 may establish that an operation occurred, but they are not automatically authoritative financial expenditure.

Use provider billing or invoice data only when it is available and reliably integrated.

Support traceable expenditure references, including:

- Source module.
- Source transaction identifier.
- Campaign or budget allocation reference.
- Transaction date.
- Currency and amount.
- Status.
- Reversal or correction reference, where applicable.

### Reconciliation rules

- Use stable source transaction identifiers.
- Make synchronization idempotent.
- Detect missing or duplicate source references.
- Reconcile reversals and corrections.
- Keep budget actuals traceable to their originating financial records.
- Distinguish estimated campaign costs from authoritative actual expenditure.
- Display the last synchronization or reconciliation status.
- Do not post accounting journals or initiate payments unless those actions already belong to an existing, explicitly supported Finance workflow.

If the Finance module does not expose an appropriate integration, implement a clean adapter interface and document which amounts remain manually entered, estimated, or unavailable.

- Enforce permissions and approval requirements.
- Prevent duplicate allocation of the same funds.
- Define how cancelled or completed activities release unused commitments.
- Keep parent and child totals consistent.
- Avoid counting the same expense at multiple levels in consolidated reporting.

Do not implement a complex cross-organization budget-transfer system unless required by the existing ERP design.

## 15. Alerts and Budget Thresholds

Implement useful budget monitoring using existing notification and scheduling infrastructure.

Support configurable thresholds, such as:

- A percentage of approved budget consumed.
- A monetary amount remaining.
- A projected or committed amount exceeding an approved limit.
- Budget expiry or period end.
- Missing or stale financial reconciliation.
- A failed or delayed expenditure synchronization.

Thresholds should be configurable within appropriate limits.

Requirements:

- Identify the budget and period concerned.
- Clearly state which amount and calculation basis triggered the alert.
- Prevent repeated notifications for the same unchanged condition.
- Support notification escalation only where existing infrastructure provides it.
- Distinguish estimated thresholds from authoritative financial thresholds.
- Avoid making unverified forecasts look like confirmed expenditure.

Do not implement advanced predictive budget forecasting in this phase.
