# Phase 05: Sales Targets, Territories, and Team Performance

## Role and Implementation Approach

Act as a senior ERP architect, business analyst, database designer, backend engineer, frontend engineer, and QA engineer.

Implement **Phase 05: Sales Targets, Territories, and Team Performance** in the existing ERP repository.

This ERP is intended to support businesses across multiple industries. Do not introduce assumptions, terminology, workflows, or data structures specific to garment manufacturing, textiles, or any single industry.

This is one phase of a larger Sales & Marketing implementation. Build this phase as a production-quality extension of the existing ERP, not as a standalone application or a separate subsystem that duplicates existing functionality.

### Sources of truth

Follow these sources in order:

1. Existing repository architecture, coding conventions, application patterns, and technical documentation.
2. The Sales & Marketing Feature Requirements Document, which defines the business scope.
3. Existing implementations from completed Sales & Marketing phases.
4. The requirements in this prompt.

Before coding, inspect the repository and identify the existing implementations of authentication, authorization, organizations or tenants, employees or users, teams, CRM, sales enquiries, quotations, pricing, Sales Orders, Finance, reporting, audit logs, and event processing.

Reuse existing functionality wherever available. Do not create duplicate systems simply because a new implementation would be easier.

If a required dependency is missing, implement only the minimum necessary within this phase, or document the dependency and its impact. Do not silently invent a parallel module.

## 1. Phase Objective

Build a comprehensive Sales Targets, Territories, and Team Performance capability that enables authorized users to:

- Define and manage sales territories, regions, and coverage assignments.
- Organize sales responsibilities across individuals and teams.
- Assign sales targets to individuals, teams, territories, and other supported organizational sales units.
- Track actual sales performance against approved targets.
- Understand target attainment, progress, gaps, and historical performance.
- Attribute sales results consistently to the appropriate representatives, teams, and territories.
- Review performance through dashboards, reports, and drill-down views.
- Maintain historical accuracy, accountability, and auditability.

The implementation must integrate with the existing CRM, enquiry, quotation, Sales Order, and financial data sources without duplicating their records or responsibilities.

## 2. Scope Boundaries

### Included in this phase

- Territory and sales coverage management.
- Sales team and representative assignment integration.
- Target creation, approval, versioning, and management.

## 4. Territory Management

Implement territory management only if equivalent functionality does not already exist.

### 4.1 Territory records

Support appropriate territory attributes, including:

- Unique identifier.
- Name and optional code.
- Description.
- Parent territory, where hierarchical territories are supported.
- Territory type or classification, where appropriate.
- Status, such as draft, active, inactive, or archived, following existing conventions.
- Effective start and end dates, where required.
- Assigned team or responsible representative, if applicable.
- Audit fields and timestamps.
- Organization or tenant scope, using existing architecture.

Territory names and structures must be configurable. Do not hardcode a fixed set of geographic regions or industry-specific categories.

### 4.2 Hierarchical territories

Where useful and consistent with the existing architecture, support parent-child territory relationships.

Examples include geographic regions, market segments, customer coverage areas, or other configurable business-defined sales areas.

Enforce data integrity:

- Prevent circular parent-child relationships.
- Prevent invalid self-parenting.
- Validate parent and child relationships within the appropriate organization or tenant.
- Prevent accidental deletion of territories with dependent assignments or historical performance.
- Define clear behavior when a parent territory is deactivated.
- Avoid double-counting performance when aggregating parent and child territories.

If the existing ERP already provides an organizational hierarchy that can serve this purpose, reuse it where appropriate.

### 4.3 Territory lifecycle

Support creating, updating, activating, deactivating, and archiving territories according to established ERP conventions.

Where historical sales records reference a territory, preserve the historical reference. Prefer deactivation or archival over destructive deletion.

Validate effective dates and prevent overlapping or conflicting assignments when the business rules prohibit them.

### 4.4 Territory assignment

Provide a configurable mechanism to assign sales representatives and teams to territories.

Support effective dates for assignments where appropriate.

If the same representative may cover multiple territories, model that relationship explicitly rather than storing a single territory field on the representative.

Where multiple representatives or teams share a territory, allow the assignment model to represent the supported coverage arrangement.

Do not create duplicate employee or user records.

## 6. Sales Target Management

Implement a configurable target-management capability.

### 6.1 Target dimensions

Where supported by the existing organizational model, targets may be assigned to:

- Individual sales representatives.
- Sales teams.
- Territories.
- Organizational sales units.
- The overall organization, where authorized and appropriate.

Targets may be defined for a combination of dimensions only when the combination has a clear business meaning.

Prevent ambiguous or duplicate target definitions for the same scope, metric, and period unless the system explicitly supports versioning or multiple target plans.

### 6.2 Target attributes

A target record or target plan should support the following concepts where relevant:

- Unique identifier.
- Target name and description.
- Target scope and assigned entity.
- Target metric.
- Target value.
- Currency, for monetary targets.
- Measurement unit, where relevant.
- Period type, such as monthly, quarterly, annually, or custom.
- Period start and end dates.
- Status and lifecycle.
- Draft and approval information.
- Version or revision information.
- Notes and supporting metadata.
- Organization or tenant scope.
- Created by, updated by, and timestamps.

Follow existing naming and data-model conventions.

Do not assume that every target is monetary. The target system must be able to support different meaningful metrics, such as order count, booked sales value, or other supported measures.

### 6.3 Supported target metrics

Create a clear, extensible metric-definition mechanism rather than scattering hardcoded metric names throughout the application.

At minimum, evaluate support for:

- Booked Sales Order Value.
- Booked Sales Order Count.
- Accepted Quotation Value.
- Accepted Quotation Count.
- Invoiced Sales Value, if a reliable Finance source exists.
- Collected Payment Value, if a reliable Finance source exists.
- Quantity or other operational sales metrics, where meaningful and supported by existing data.

Only expose metrics whose data sources and calculation rules can be implemented reliably.

For every metric, define:

- What the metric measures.
- Its authoritative source.
- Which statuses qualify.
- Its date basis.
- Its currency and unit rules.
- How cancellations, reversals, returns, credit notes, and corrections are handled.
- How duplicate or repeated source events are prevented from inflating results.
- Whether it is suitable for target comparison.

**Do not treat quotations as booked sales or recognized revenue.** Accepted quotation value and booked Sales Order value are distinct metrics.

Do not describe invoiced sales as recognized revenue unless the existing Finance rules support that interpretation.

Do not treat booked order value, invoiced value, and collected cash as interchangeable.

### 6.4 Target periods

Support the period types required by the business and existing architecture.

Provide consistent period boundaries and date calculations. Handle leap years, varying month lengths, time zones, and the organization's configured fiscal calendar if one exists.

If the ERP supports fiscal periods, reuse those definitions instead of introducing a competing calendar.

Define whether actuals are calculated using:

- Transaction date.
- Order confirmation date.
- Invoice date.
- Payment date.
- Another explicitly configured business date.

The date basis must be specific to the metric and documented.

Do not silently switch between date fields across dashboards and reports.

### 6.5 Target lifecycle and approvals

Support an appropriate lifecycle, such as:

- Draft.
- Submitted for approval.
- Approved.
- Active.
- Rejected.
- Closed.
- Archived.

Adapt the actual statuses to existing workflow conventions.

Where approval functionality already exists, reuse it. Otherwise, implement a minimal approval mechanism only if required by the repository's business rules.

Requirements:

- Only authorized users can create or edit target plans.
- Only authorized approvers can approve or reject plans.
- Approved targets should not be silently modified.
- Changes to approved targets must create a traceable revision or use an explicit amendment process.
- Preserve the prior approved version and its effective dates.
- Prevent unauthorized activation or retrospective alteration.
- Define behavior for target changes after a period has started.
- Record approval decisions, timestamps, actors, and relevant comments where supported.

### 6.6 Target allocation and distribution

Allow authorized users to assign targets directly and, where useful, allocate a higher-level target among lower-level entities.

For example, a team target may be distributed among team members.

Support the allocation rules appropriate to the business:

- Manual allocation.
- Equal allocation, if appropriate.
- Percentage-based allocation.
- Value-based allocation.

Do not assume every target can be distributed using the same method.

Validate allocation totals and rounding rules. When allocations are expected to sum to a parent target, make that constraint explicit and enforce it.

If a target is allocated across multiple dimensions, ensure the same amount is not counted repeatedly in consolidated reporting.

Keep allocation rules transparent and auditable.

## 7. Actual Sales Performance Calculation

This is a critical component of Phase 05.

Implement a consistent, testable mechanism for calculating actual performance against target values.

### 7.1 Authoritative data sources

Use the existing modules as the authoritative source for their respective records:

- Accepted quotation records from the existing quotation implementation.
- Confirmed or otherwise qualifying Sales Orders from the existing Sales Order module.
- Invoices and credit notes from Finance, if available.
- Payments and reversals from Finance, if available.
- Existing CRM ownership or assignment records where relevant to sales attribution.

Do not create shadow copies of Sales Orders, quotations, invoices, payments, or employees merely to support reporting.

Use existing APIs, services, domain events, reporting views, or other approved integration patterns.

### 7.2 Calculation rules

For each metric, establish and document:

- Eligible transaction statuses.
- Applicable date field.
- Treatment of cancelled transactions.
- Treatment of rejected, expired, or superseded quotations.
- Treatment of cancelled or amended orders.
- Treatment of returns, credit notes, refunds, and payment reversals where relevant.
- Currency conversion rules, if cross-currency aggregation is supported.
- Rounding and precision rules.
- Duplicate prevention and idempotency rules.
- Behavior when source records are corrected after initial processing.

A quotation must not contribute to booked Sales Order value simply because it was created, sent, or accepted.

Do not include cancelled transactions as positive sales without an explicit, documented business rule.

If an order is amended, determine whether the actual reflects the current eligible value or a transaction-based net value, according to the existing Sales Order model. Avoid counting both the original and amended value.

### 7.3 Calculation architecture

Use a shared service, query layer, or reporting abstraction for metric calculation.

Avoid implementing different definitions of the same metric independently across:

- Dashboard widgets.
- Target detail screens.
- Team reports.
- Territory reports.
- Export endpoints.

For larger datasets, use efficient database queries, pre-aggregations, or existing analytics infrastructure as appropriate.

If calculations are cached or processed asynchronously:

- Define freshness expectations.
- Prevent duplicate event processing.
- Support safe retries.
- Provide a reconciliation or refresh mechanism.
- Avoid displaying stale data as if it were current.
- Indicate the last calculated or refreshed timestamp where useful.

For small datasets, prefer a straightforward reliable implementation over unnecessary infrastructure.

## 8. Sales Attribution and Credit Assignment

Define explicit rules for assigning sales performance to representatives, teams, and territories.

This is essential for trustworthy performance reporting.

### 8.1 Attribution sources

Reuse existing CRM ownership, quotation ownership, Sales Order attribution, team membership, and territory assignments where available.

Do not infer sales ownership from the currently logged-in user or from whichever user happens to open a record.

### 8.2 Attribution rules

Establish and document the applicable policy for:

- Individual representative credit.
- Team credit.
- Territory credit.
- Records involving multiple representatives.
- Shared or split credit, if supported.
- Changes in ownership or team membership.
- Transfers between territories.
- Historical performance for former or inactive representatives.

If split credit is supported, validate that the total credit does not exceed the permitted amount for the relevant metric and transaction.

If split credit is not supported in this phase, explicitly document the single-owner attribution rule and its limitations rather than implying that shared credit works.

### 8.3 Historical integrity

Do not allow current assignments to silently rewrite historical results.

Define the effective-date and snapshot rules needed to preserve accurate historical attribution.

For example, if a representative moves to another team, earlier eligible transactions should remain attributed according to the configured policy, not automatically move to the new team.

If a source record changes ownership after the sale, apply the documented correction policy and preserve an audit trail.

Ensure that individual, team, and territory reports do not accidentally count the same transaction multiple times when consolidated.

## 9. Target Attainment and Progress

For each supported target metric, calculate and display:

- Target value.
- Actual value.
- Remaining gap.
- Attainment percentage.
- Period start and end.
- Time elapsed or remaining, where meaningful.
- Calculation or refresh timestamp.
- Applicable scope, metric, currency, and unit.

Use a consistent calculation:

Attainment percentage = actual value / target value × 100

Handle zero targets, missing targets, negative adjustments, and undefined cases safely. Never allow division-by-zero errors or misleading infinite percentages.

Define how the UI presents overachievement, underachievement, and missing or unavailable data.

Do not present an unapproved draft target as an approved target.

Do not compare incompatible currencies, units, periods, or metric definitions.

If an aggregate combines currencies, use an established and documented conversion policy. If no reliable conversion policy exists, show separate currency totals rather than inventing a conversion.
