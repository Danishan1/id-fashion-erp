# Phase 13: Campaign Attribution and ROI Analytics

## 1. Objective

Implement a production-oriented, industry-agnostic **Campaign Attribution and ROI Analytics** capability within the existing Sales & Marketing ERP module.

The module must enable authorized users to measure and analyze how marketing campaigns contribute to business outcomes, including leads, enquiries, quotations, orders, revenue, and other supported conversion events.

It must connect authoritative records from the existing ERP modules with campaign interactions and marketing expenditure to produce transparent, reproducible, and auditable performance measurements.

The solution must be industry-agnostic. Do not introduce garment-, textile-, manufacturing-, or other industry-specific assumptions, entities, metrics, or workflows.

The core objectives are to:

- Define and manage attribution models.
- Capture and normalize supported marketing touchpoints.
- Connect touchpoints to existing campaigns and canonical business entities.
- Track supported conversion events.
- Attribute outcomes according to configurable, documented rules.
- Calculate campaign performance, acquisition costs, return on investment, and related metrics where the required data exists.
- Reconcile marketing expenditure with authoritative financial records from Phase 12.
- Provide dashboards, reports, drill-downs, and historical comparisons.
- Preserve data provenance, model versions, and reproducible attribution results.

**Critical principle:** attribution indicates how a defined model allocates credit for an outcome. It does not, by itself, prove that a campaign caused the outcome.

## 4. Attribution Data Model and Concepts

Keep the following concepts distinct:

- **Campaign:** the existing marketing initiative.
- **Touchpoint:** an observed or imported interaction associated with a campaign or channel.
- **Conversion:** an authoritative business outcome or defined conversion event.
- **Attribution model:** the rule used to allocate credit across eligible touchpoints.
- **Attribution result:** a versioned calculation connecting a conversion to eligible touchpoints.
- **Financial outcome:** an authoritative revenue or financial measure linked to a conversion.
- **Marketing expenditure:** a cost sourced from Phase 12 or another verified financial source.

Use existing entities wherever possible.

Potential new entities, if not already available, may include:

- Touchpoint event.
- Conversion event or conversion reference.
- Attribution model and version.
- Attribution calculation run.
- Attribution result or credit allocation.
- Data-source synchronization checkpoint.
- Attribution data-quality record.

These are conceptual possibilities, not a requirement to create every table.

Avoid creating duplicate campaigns, contacts, leads, orders, invoices, or financial transactions.

## 5. Attribution Model Management

Implement a configurable attribution model registry.

Depending on the architecture, supported models may include:

- First-touch attribution.
- Last-touch attribution.
- Linear attribution.
- Position-based attribution.
- Time-decay attribution.
- Rule-based single-touch or multi-touch attribution.

Implement only models whose rules can be defined, tested, and explained reliably.

### Model configuration

Support appropriate settings, such as:

- Model name and description.
- Model type.
- Attribution window.
- Eligible touchpoint types.
- Eligible channels.
- Eligible campaign statuses or categories, where relevant.
- Position-based weighting parameters.
- Time-decay parameters.
- Direct-traffic handling rules.
- Conversion event types.
- Revenue or conversion-credit basis.
- Model status.
- Effective dates.
- Version and revision history.
- Owner and approval information where required.

Validate configuration on the server.

Require model weights to be valid and normalized according to the chosen algorithm.

Reject invalid parameters and unsupported combinations.

### Versioning

Once an attribution model has produced results, preserve the model version used for those calculations.

Changes to the model must not silently rewrite historical results. Recalculation must create a new, identifiable run or result version.

Do not make an attribution model active until required validation and approval have completed.

## 6. Touchpoint Capture and Normalization

Integrate with actual touchpoint sources already available through Phase 10 and existing ERP infrastructure.

Potential touchpoints include:

- Campaign message submission or provider acceptance.
- Confirmed message delivery.
- Email opens or clicks, where available and reliable.
- SMS or messaging interactions, where provided.
- Advertising impressions and clicks, where supported by provider integrations.
- Website visits and tracked campaign-link interactions, where an appropriate tracking mechanism exists.
- Form submissions.
- Campaign responses.
- Enquiries and lead creation.
- Other verified engagement events exposed by existing systems.

Do not treat every event as equivalent evidence.

For example:

- A submitted message is not a confirmed delivery.
- A confirmed delivery is not proof of a person reading it.
- An email open may be affected by privacy protections, image proxies, or automated scanners.
- An advertising click is not necessarily a qualified lead.
- A conversion associated with a campaign is not necessarily caused by that campaign.

### Normalized touchpoint fields

Store only the information required for attribution, such as:

- Unique event identifier.
- Event type.
- Event timestamp and time zone basis.
- Campaign reference, when known.
- Channel and provider references.
- Provider event or interaction identifier.
- Canonical CRM entity reference, when reliably identified.
- Source record reference.
- Consent or tracking eligibility metadata, where applicable.
- Ingestion timestamp.
- Data-source provenance.
- Processing status.
- Relevant deduplication key.

Use canonical references rather than copying full CRM records into analytics tables.

Deduplicate using stable event identifiers and provider-specific semantics.

If identity is unknown or ambiguous, retain the event only where privacy policy permits and clearly mark it as unattributed. Do not guess a person's identity from weak matching signals.

## 7. Conversion Tracking and Authoritative Outcomes

Define a reusable conversion event framework based on authoritative existing business records.

Supported conversion events may include:

- New enquiry.
- Qualified lead.
- Accepted quotation.
- Sales Order creation or confirmation.
- Invoice issuance.
- Recognized revenue, where supported by Finance.
- Completed transaction.
- Other explicitly configured business outcomes.

These events must not be treated as interchangeable.

For each conversion type, define:

- Authoritative source module.
- Canonical source identifier.
- Event timestamp and relevant business date.
- Conversion status.
- Value and currency, where applicable.
- Reversal or cancellation behavior.
- Eligibility for attribution.
- Data-source provenance.

Use the existing authoritative record lifecycle. Do not infer that an order was completed because a quotation was accepted or that revenue was recognized because an order was created.

### Conversion deduplication

Prevent the same business outcome from being counted multiple times due to:

- Repeated source events.
- Duplicate webhooks.
- Retried synchronization jobs.
- Status changes.
- Multiple event types referencing the same canonical outcome.
- Repeated attribution processing.

A single underlying order or invoice may legitimately be related to multiple funnel events, but reports must define whether they measure distinct conversions, funnel-stage progression, order count, invoice count, or revenue.

Avoid adding the same financial outcome multiple times simply because it appears in several modules.

## 8. Identity Resolution and Privacy

Connect touchpoints to existing CRM and transaction records using authorized, reliable identifiers.

Prefer deterministic relationships such as:

- Existing CRM contact identifiers.
- Existing lead or enquiry identifiers.
- Existing account or customer references.
- Verified provider identifiers already mapped to canonical CRM records.
- Existing campaign tracking identifiers that can be linked reliably to an outcome.

Do not introduce probabilistic identity stitching unless the repository already provides a governed and privacy-compliant mechanism.

### Privacy safeguards

- Respect applicable consent and tracking preferences.
- Respect opt-outs and suppression rules where relevant.
- Do not assume consent to marketing communications implies consent to all analytics tracking.
- Do not assume permission to use personal data for attribution without the applicable policy or legal basis.
- Minimize stored personal data.
- Apply tenant isolation and field-level authorization.
- Use pseudonymous or aggregated identifiers where appropriate.
- Define retention and deletion behavior for touchpoints and attribution data.
- Support correction or removal processes required by existing privacy controls.
- Avoid placing personal identifiers in URLs, analytics events, or logs unless explicitly justified and protected.

Where tracking consent or identity data is unavailable, document the limitation and avoid claiming complete attribution coverage.

Do not create a duplicate consent-management system.

## 9. Attribution Windows and Eligibility

Support configurable attribution windows for each model or conversion type.

For example, a model may consider eligible touchpoints occurring within a defined number of days before the conversion. The duration must be configurable rather than universally hard-coded.

Define:

- Whether the conversion timestamp is included.
- Whether touchpoints after conversion are excluded.
- Whether the window is based on event time or ingestion time.
- How late-arriving events are handled.
- How time zones and daylight-saving transitions are handled.
- Which event types are eligible.
- How repeated touchpoints from the same campaign or channel are treated.
- How direct or unattributed traffic is handled.
- Whether internal, test, bot, or invalid events are excluded.

Use event time for attribution where trustworthy, while retaining ingestion time for operational diagnostics.

Define deterministic behavior for boundary conditions.

Do not change historical eligibility rules silently when configuration changes.

## 10. Attribution Algorithms

Implement the chosen attribution algorithms as isolated, testable services.

### First-touch

Allocate eligible conversion credit to the earliest eligible touchpoint under the configured model rules.

### Last-touch

Allocate eligible conversion credit to the latest eligible touchpoint before the conversion, subject to configured exclusions.

### Linear

Distribute credit evenly across eligible touchpoints according to the documented deduplication and eligibility policy.

### Position-based

Allocate configured weights to the first and last eligible touchpoints and distribute the remaining weight across intermediate touchpoints, as configured.

### Time-decay

Allocate credit according to the configured decay function and the age of each eligible touchpoint relative to the conversion.

Document exact formulas, tie-breaking rules, rounding behavior, and treatment of a conversion with no eligible touchpoints.

Do not implement a model by name alone without a clearly defined calculation.

### Calculation integrity

For every attribution result:

- Record the model and model version.
- Record the conversion source reference.
- Record the eligible touchpoints used.
- Record the attribution window.
- Record the calculation timestamp and run identifier.
- Preserve allocated conversion credit.
- Preserve allocated monetary value where applicable.
- Track exclusions and reason codes where useful.
- Ensure allocated weights sum to the intended total within the documented precision rules.

If no touchpoints qualify, classify the conversion as unattributed rather than inventing campaign credit.

Do not silently assign unattributed conversions to a campaign.

## 11. Revenue Attribution and Financial Integrity

Use authoritative financial sources for revenue-based attribution.

Distinguish clearly between:

- Quoted value.
- Accepted order value.
- Booked sales.
- Invoiced amount.
- Recognized revenue, where available.
- Collected cash, where available.
- Refunds, credit notes, cancellations, and reversals.

These metrics are not interchangeable.

Select the revenue basis explicitly for each report or attribution configuration, and label it clearly.

### Financial requirements

- Use authoritative Finance and Sales Order services.
- Preserve source currency and monetary precision.
- Reuse existing currency conversion and exchange-rate services.
- Define the exchange-rate date and reporting-currency basis.
- Never aggregate currencies without a defined conversion basis.
- Handle partial invoices, partial refunds, credit notes, and cancellations according to source-module semantics.
- Prevent duplicate counting across orders, invoices, and accounting records.
- Keep attributed revenue separate from actual marketing expenditure.
- Keep attributed revenue separate from profit or contribution margin unless authoritative cost data supports those calculations.

### Revenue allocation

If a conversion has a monetary value, allocate it according to the selected attribution weights.

Ensure that total allocated value reconciles to the eligible conversion value within the documented rounding rules.

If a financial outcome changes due to an authoritative correction, reversal, or refund, support controlled recalculation and preserve the history of the prior attribution run.

Do not modify the underlying order, invoice, or accounting records as part of attribution.

## 13. ROI, ROAS, and Marketing KPIs

Implement performance metrics only when their underlying data is available and the calculation is defined.

### Return on marketing investment

A basic revenue-based ROI calculation may be:

\[
\text{ROI} =
\frac{\text{Attributed Revenue} - \text{Marketing Cost}}
{\text{Marketing Cost}}
\times 100\%
\]

This is a revenue-based measure, not profit-based ROI. Label it clearly.

Do not describe revenue-based ROI as profit ROI.

If marketing cost is zero, unavailable, or not reconciled, do not return a misleading percentage. Display an appropriate unavailable or not-applicable status.

### Return on advertising spend

Where advertising spend and attributed revenue are available:

\[
\text{ROAS} =
\frac{\text{Attributed Revenue}}
{\text{Advertising Spend}}
\]

Clearly identify the attribution model, revenue basis, reporting currency, and period.

### Other potential KPIs

Where supported by reliable data, provide:

- Campaign reach or impressions.
- Clicks and engagement.
- Click-through rate.
- Conversion count and conversion rate.
- Lead or enquiry acquisition cost.
- Customer acquisition cost, only when the definition and unique-customer source are reliable.
- Cost per conversion.
- Attributed order count.
- Attributed revenue.
- Revenue per campaign.
- Marketing spend utilization.
- Unattributed conversion rate.
- Attribution coverage.
- Funnel progression metrics.

Define the numerator, denominator, time window, and source for every metric.

Do not calculate metrics from missing data by substituting arbitrary zero values.

Do not compare unlike metrics without making their different bases clear.

## 15. Recalculation, Historical Results, and Reproducibility

Implement a controlled attribution calculation pipeline.

Support:

- Initial calculation.
- Incremental processing of new eligible events.
- Reprocessing after late-arriving events.
- Recalculation after a conversion correction or reversal.
- Recalculation under a new attribution model version.
- Historical comparisons between model versions.
- Failure tracking and safe retries.

Each run should record:

- Run identifier.
- Model and model version.
- Conversion or date range processed.
- Source-data checkpoint or watermark, where supported.
- Calculation start and completion time.
- Number of records processed.
- Number of successful, failed, and skipped records.
- Relevant validation or reconciliation warnings.

Do not overwrite historical results without retaining sufficient history to understand what changed.

When late-arriving events alter the result, make the recalculation traceable and distinguish updated results from the prior run.

Prevent duplicate calculations and concurrent jobs from producing inconsistent or duplicated credit allocations.

Use existing background jobs and event infrastructure.
