# Phase 03: Pricing, Price Lists, and Discount Engine

## 1. Role and Objective

Act as a senior ERP architect, full-stack engineer, database engineer, pricing-domain specialist, and QA engineer.

Implement **Phase 03: Pricing, Price Lists, and Discount Engine** in the existing ERP repository.

The objective is to build a reusable, configurable pricing engine that determines applicable prices and discounts for products and services based on customer, transaction context, quantity, date, currency, sales channel, and other supported business rules.

The engine must support multiple industries, business models, organizations, products, services, and sales channels. Do not hardcode assumptions for garment manufacturing, textiles, retail, wholesale, distribution, or any other single industry.

This must be an extension of the existing ERP, not a separate pricing application.

The result must be production-oriented, secure, testable, auditable, and integrated with the existing quotation workflow.

## 4. Pricing Engine Architecture

Create a central pricing service that accepts a pricing request and returns a validated, explainable pricing result.

The engine must be deterministic: the same inputs and the same applicable rule/configuration versions must produce the same result.

### 4.1 Pricing request context

Support relevant fields such as:

- Organization or tenant context derived securely from the authenticated request.
- Product or service identifier.
- Quantity.
- Unit of measure.
- Customer, account, or customer group.
- Applicable price list.
- Transaction date.
- Currency.
- Sales channel.
- Sales region or territory, if supported.
- Contract or negotiated-pricing reference, if supported.
- Applicable commercial context.
- Requested discount context, where permitted.

Only use fields that are meaningful in the existing architecture. Do not create duplicate master-data models just to populate these fields.

Validate required inputs and ensure that every referenced record is accessible to the requesting tenant.

### 4.2 Pricing response

Return a structured result containing applicable fields such as:

- Product/service identifier.
- Source price list and version.
- Base unit price.
- Selected pricing rule.
- Quantity tier or pricing basis.
- Applied discount rules.
- Discount amounts.
- Net unit price.
- Extended line amount.
- Currency.
- Quantity and unit of measure.
- Effective date.
- Calculation and rounding details.
- Warnings or validation issues.
- Human-readable pricing explanation.
- Rule/configuration references needed for auditability.

Never return a price without being able to explain which source and rules determined it, except for explicitly supported manual-price workflows.

Do not expose confidential pricing data from other customers or tenants.

## 6. Price Resolution and Precedence

Implement and document a deterministic price resolution algorithm.

Potential price sources include:

1. Explicitly authorized manual price override.
2. Valid customer-specific negotiated price or contract rate.
3. Applicable customer-specific price list.
4. Applicable customer-group price list.
5. Applicable sales-channel or regional price list.
6. Applicable general/default price list.
7. A supported standard catalog price, if one exists in the current architecture.

This is a proposed precedence model, not a requirement to implement every source. Adapt it to the repository's actual data model and business rules.

### Required behavior

- Select only prices effective for the transaction date.
- Validate customer, product, currency, unit, and tenant compatibility.
- Use explicit priorities to resolve legitimate overlaps.
- Detect ambiguous or conflicting matches and return a meaningful validation error rather than selecting an arbitrary price.
- Apply documented fallback behavior when no applicable price is found.
- Do not silently substitute currencies or units.
- Do not use current prices to reconstruct a historical transaction.
- Do not allow a lower-priority rule to override a higher-priority source without an explicit configured rule.
- Provide a pricing explanation identifying the selected source and why competing sources were not selected, where practical.

Write the precedence algorithm in technical documentation and cover it with automated tests.

## 7. Quantity-Based and Tiered Pricing

Support quantity-dependent prices where required.

Possible pricing models:

- Fixed unit price.
- Quantity-break pricing.
- Graduated or progressive quantity pricing, if the architecture and requirements support it.
- Minimum order quantity.
- Price per unit of measure.
- Customer-specific quantity tiers.

Clearly distinguish:

- **All-units tier pricing:** the selected tier price applies to the entire eligible quantity.
- **Graduated pricing:** different portions of the quantity may be priced at different tier rates.

Do not treat these models as interchangeable.

Requirements:

- Validate tier boundaries.
- Prevent unintended overlapping tiers.
- Define inclusive/exclusive quantity boundaries.
- Support fractional quantities when the product/service and unit permit them.
- Use consistent decimal arithmetic.
- Test exact boundary values.
- Return the selected tier or tier calculation in the pricing explanation.

Do not introduce industry-specific minimums or packaging assumptions.

Build a reusable discount rule engine separate from price list maintenance, while integrating both into the overall pricing calculation.

### 8.1 Discount rule fields

Support relevant configuration fields such as:

- Rule name and code.
- Description.
- Status.
- Discount type.
- Percentage or fixed amount.
- Applicable product/service or category.
- Applicable customer or customer group.
- Applicable price list.
- Minimum quantity.
- Minimum transaction value.
- Currency.
- Validity period.
- Sales channel.
- Rule priority.
- Stacking policy.
- Maximum discount limit.
- Required approval threshold.
- Eligibility conditions.
- Exclusion conditions.
- Usage limits only where reliable tracking and transactional enforcement are supported.
- Created and updated metadata.

Reuse existing segmentation, customer-group, category, and authorization mechanisms where available.

Do not build a full marketing campaign system in this phase.

### 8.2 Discount types

Support, where applicable:

- Percentage discount.
- Fixed amount per unit.
- Fixed amount per eligible line.
- Document-level fixed discount.
- Document-level percentage discount.
- Quantity-based discount.
- Customer-specific negotiated discount.
- Manually entered discount subject to permissions and constraints.

Clearly define the calculation base for each discount type.

For fixed-amount discounts, specify whether the amount is per unit, per line, or per document. Never infer this silently.

### 8.3 Eligibility and exclusions

Evaluate discount rules against the actual transaction context.

Potential conditions include:

- Product or service.
- Product/service category.
- Customer or customer group.
- Quantity.
- Transaction subtotal.
- Currency.
- Sales channel.
- Region.
- Transaction date.
- Applicable price list.
- Existing discount state.

The engine must:

- Validate all rule references.
- Ignore inactive or out-of-date rules.
- Apply exclusions deterministically.
- Avoid evaluating unrelated or inaccessible tenant data.
- Explain why an eligible-looking rule was not applied when practical.
- Reject unsupported conditions instead of silently ignoring them.

Do not implement a general-purpose arbitrary-code rule engine. Use safe, typed, validated rule conditions consistent with the existing architecture.

### 8.4 Discount stacking and precedence

Define explicit stacking behavior.

Support suitable policies such as:

- Non-stackable: only the highest-precedence eligible discount applies.
- Stackable: eligible discounts may be combined under a documented calculation sequence.
- Exclusive groups: only one rule in a defined group may apply.
- Manual override: permitted only under the appropriate authorization and approval policy.

Requirements:

- Use deterministic rule priority and tie-breaking.
- Prevent the same rule from being applied twice.
- Prevent circular rule dependencies.
- Define whether sequential percentage discounts are applied to the original amount or the progressively discounted amount.
- Define how fixed discounts interact with percentage discounts.
- Enforce configured maximum discounts.
- Prevent discounts from creating impermissible negative prices or totals.
- Respect explicit policy on whether discounts may exceed gross margin or other financial limits; do not assume cost or margin data exists.
- Return a complete discount breakdown.

Document the order of operations and test it thoroughly.

### 8.5 Manual discounts and overrides

Allow manual price or discount overrides only through controlled workflows.

Requirements:

- Enforce specific permissions.
- Capture the original calculated price and the overridden price.
- Require a reason where policy dictates.
- Record the user, timestamp, and affected transaction.
- Apply configured approval rules.
- Preserve the override snapshot on issued quotations and finalized transactions.
- Never silently replace a manual override during recalculation.
- Provide a clear action to recalculate from the current pricing rules when authorized.

Do not treat an override as a change to the underlying master price list.

## 9. Pricing Calculation Order

Define one authoritative server-side calculation pipeline.

A suitable starting point is:

1. Validate transaction context.
2. Resolve the applicable price source and version.
3. Resolve the applicable unit price.
4. Calculate the pre-discount extended amount.
5. Identify eligible discount rules.
6. Resolve discount stacking and precedence.
7. Apply line-level discounts.
8. Apply permitted document-level discounts using a documented allocation method.
9. Calculate applicable taxes through the existing tax engine, in the order required by the applicable tax rules.
10. Apply permitted fees or charges through existing mechanisms.
11. Apply defined rounding rules.
12. Return the final calculation and explanation.

The exact sequence must be compatible with the existing ERP's tax and quotation calculation architecture. Do not assume that all jurisdictions calculate taxes on the same basis.

Requirements:

- Use decimal-safe arithmetic, not binary floating-point for financial calculations.
- Define monetary precision and rounding consistently.
- Avoid repeated rounding at intermediate stages unless the documented business or tax rules require it.
- Prevent frontend-supplied totals from being trusted.
- Make recalculation deterministic.
- Keep calculation logic in reusable backend services rather than duplicating it across screens and endpoints.
- Add tests for rounding, discounts, tax interactions, and boundary conditions.

- Avoid loading all price lists or rules for every request.
- Cache pricing data only if cache invalidation and version consistency are handled correctly.
